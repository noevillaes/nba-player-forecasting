    How it works

    Every morning a scheduled job downloads the schedule, rosters and last night's box scores, forecasts the day's games and scores yesterday's. Everything is published before tip-off, so every forecast has a timestamp.

    Simulated results. The backtest forecasts for March 2026 were made after those games were played, to test the pipeline before the season. They are not part of the live track record.

              Player forecasts

              For every player on each roster, the model forecasts 16 box-score stats. For the 15 counting stats it doesn't predict one number but a full probability distribution: a LightGBM model estimates the average outcome, and a negative binomial distribution fitted on last season describes the spread around it. The number shown is the median of that distribution and the range holds at least 80% of it. Both are whole numbers, because nobody scores 23.4 points. Minutes come from quantile models at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles.

              The model sees each player's recent form at several speeds: averages over his last 3, 5, 10 and 20 games, smoothed averages that fade older games gradually, and per-minute production over the last 82 games. Short windows catch role changes within days, like a starter moved to the bench. Long ones keep a hot or cold streak in proportion. All of them only use games played before the one being forecast.

              Points, rebounds and assists are then reconciled with what the team is expected to produce, so a roster full of scorers can't add up to a total no team would reach. The multiplier shown above each box score is the adjustment on points.

              That's also how it handles who's missing. Players listed out or doubtful on the morning injury report get no forecast and don't count toward the team's total, so what they usually produce goes to the teammates who do play. On this season's games so far, that cut the error on points from 4.39 to 4.38.

              Each box score also shows why: the average outcome for points, rebounds and assists, starting from a typical player and broken into what moved it, like recent production, minutes and role, or the opponent.

              Ranges that stay honest

              Each distribution is checked against last season, which the model never trained on: if what it calls the 90th percentile turned out to be the 89th, the 89th is used. Because the ranges are whole numbers they hold at least 80% of the outcomes, and more for low-count stats like steals and blocks, where a range can't be narrower than 0 to 1.

              The minutes range is calibrated with conformalized quantile regression. Once a week it's redone with the last 28 days of live forecasts, which the model never saw, and each end of the range is adjusted on its own, so misses don't pile up on one side.

              A model that learns during the season

              Once a month a challenger model is trained with everything up to two weeks ago, this season included. Both models then forecast those last two weeks, which neither has seen, and the challenger only takes over if it makes less error. Each forecast records which version made it.

              Game forecasts

              Each team carries three ratings, offense, defense and pace, that move a little after every game, the way an Elo rating does, and drift back toward average between seasons. A team's expected score is the expected pace times its offense against the opponent's defense, plus home court and an adjustment for back-to-backs. Over seven seasons it had never seen, it missed the final margin by 10.9 points on average, against 11.2 for the 10-game formula it replaced on Nov 10. Win probabilities are calibrated with Platt scaling. A Brier score of 0.250 is what a coin flip would get.

              What it's compared against

              Every player stat is compared with that player's average over his last 10 games, a simple forecast that's hard to beat.

                Known limits

                • Availability comes from the morning injury report. Players ruled out later, or listed as questionable who end up sitting, still get a forecast. They show as DNP and aren't counted in the errors.
                • Individual matchups aren't modeled.
                • Rookies without NBA games have no history to forecast from.

                Data

                Historical box scores come from the Kaggle dataset Historical NBA Data and Player Box Scores (1947 to present). Live data comes from the NBA stats API through nba_api. The backtest set holds forecasts for March 2026 games, made after the fact to test the pipeline before the season started.

                Every morning

                1. Pull new dataSchedule, rosters and last night's box scores from the NBA stats API.
                2. Build featuresRolling player and team features from every game played so far.
                3. Recalibrate rangesOnce a week, the minutes ranges, with the last 28 days of live errors.
                4. Forecast players16 stats per player with 80% ranges, without the players ruled out, reconciled with the team scores.
                5. Forecast gamesTeam scores, total, margin and win probability.
                6. Blend with the marketGame forecasts are combined with the morning's sportsbook lines, weighted by how each has done this season; player points follow their team's blended score.
                7. Score yesterdayYesterday's forecasts are compared with the real box scores.
                8. PublishThe forecasts are published and this site rebuilds a few minutes later.
                9. RetrainOnce a month, a challenger trained with this season's games replaces the current model only if it wins.
